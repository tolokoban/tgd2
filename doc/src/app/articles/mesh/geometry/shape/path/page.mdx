# [TgdGeometryPath](docs/classes/TgdGeometryPath.html)

<Demo />

A shape filled from an [SVG path](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/SVG/Reference/Attribute/d).
It is flat (Z = 0), unless you give it a `thickness`.

```ts
const geometry = new TgdGeometryPath("M0,0 L4,0 L4,4 L0,4 Z M1,1 L3,1 L2,3 Z")
```

The demo shows the same SVG path four times:

1. Flat.
2. With a `thickness` of 4.
3. With a thickness and a round `bevel` of 8 segments.
4. With a thickness and a concave `bevel` of 32 segments and a `profileShape` of 0.125.

Use the controls to switch between two paths and between the two `fillRule`:
the ghost looks the same with both rules, because its eyes turn in the other direction,
but the middle circle of the target disappears with `"nonzero"`, because all its circles turn in the same direction.

All the SVG path commands are supported, with their lowercase (relative) versions:
`M` (move), `L` (line), `H` (horizontal line), `V` (vertical line),
`Q` and `T` (quadratic Bézier curve), `C` and `S` (cubic Bézier curve),
`A` (elliptical arc) and `Z` (close).
Each subpath is a closed contour, even without `Z`.
Holes depend on the `fillRule` option, like the SVG [fill-rule](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/SVG/Reference/Attribute/fill-rule) attribute.

Options:

- `curveSegments`: number of segments used to approximate each Bézier curve,
  or each quarter turn of an arc (default to `16`).
- `flipY`: the Y axis points down in SVG, but up in WebGL. Set it to `true` to see
  the shape as in an SVG image (default to `false`).
- `fillRule`: which parts of the path are inside the shape (default to `"evenodd"`):
  - `"evenodd"`: a contour inside an odd number of other contours is a hole.
  - `"nonzero"`: contours turning in the same direction are merged, and a contour
    turning the other way inside them is a hole. Use it for font glyphs, whose contours
    often overlap (the bar and the stem of a "T" are often two overlapping rectangles).

  With both rules, the contours can cross themselves and each other:
  the geometry only keeps the outline of the filled area, so there are no hidden faces inside the volume.
- `thickness`: if greater than 0, the shape is extruded along Z, from `-thickness / 2`
  to `+thickness / 2` (default to `0`, a flat shape at Z = 0).
- `bevel`: rounds the edges of the front and back faces, like the Bevel modifier of Blender
  (only with a `thickness`):
  - `width`: bevel amount, no more than half the thickness. Keep it smaller than the
    details of the shape (thin parts, small holes...), or the faces will overlap themselves.
  - `segments`: number of segments of the curved edges.
  - `profileShape`: superellipse factor, `0.25` for a straight chamfer, `0.5` for a
    circular arc (default), towards `1` for a square corner, and towards `0` for a concave one.

The geometry provides `POSITION`, `NORMAL` and `TEXCOORD_0` attributes.
On the front and back faces, the UVs go from `[0, 1]` (bottom left) to `[1, 0]` (top right)
over the bounding box of the shape.
On the sides, U goes from 0 to 1 along the contour, and V from 1 (front) to 0 (back).
The normals of the sides are smooth along the curves, but sharp on corners of more than 30°.
